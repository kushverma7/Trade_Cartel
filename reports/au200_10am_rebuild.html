The 10 AM candle carries no directional information

A reported profit factor of 5.03 was a bug in my simulator. The live result of 0.444 was correct. This is the rebuild, the retraction, and the three tests that close the question.

Verdict — nothing found

Across 10,530 tested configurations in three independent families, on up to six years of AU200 5-minute data, zero cleared their multiple-testing significance bar. The 10:00 candle's gross expectancy is approximately zero at every horizon from five minutes to the close, before any cost is charged. There is no edge being destroyed by costs here; there is no edge.

What went wrong the first time

The first run of the rebuilt sweep immediately produced a top-18 block showing PF 99.000 and a 100.0% win rate at t = +58.23. That is BUG-040: fading an up-gap goes short, but the reference level sits below the entry, on the profitable side, so every trade “stopped out” instantly at a gain. The diagnostic tell is worth keeping — all nine exit methods returned byte-identical results, meaning the exit was never binding.

It is now blocked in code, not in my memory. R8: a stop must lie strictly adverse to the entry, or the cell is discarded. R9: metrics() refuses to return any result with a win rate above 90% or below 10%, or a profit factor above 20. A sweep can no longer rank an absurd cell at all.

Why no amount of tuning fixes it

  1. Gross expectancy is zero. With costs switched off entirely, the 10:00 candle's direction is worth between −0.26 and +0.50 points depending on the holding horizon. Every t-statistic is below 1.5 in absolute value, and the sign flips between horizons. This is not an edge being eaten by the spread — there is nothing to eat.
  2. Directional accuracy never beats chance. Measured against the next 1, 2, 3, 6, 12, 24 and 71 bars: 46.05%, 48.87%, 49.42%, 48.59%, 47.42%, 48.45%, 50.46%. The one statistically significant reading is below 50% — mild mean reversion over the first five minutes, worth +0.27 points gross against a 2-point cost.
  3. The search loses to coin flips. The best of 2,620 real cells reached t = +0.70 in sample. Twelve identical searches over randomised candle directions returned maxima of +0.46, +0.55, +0.56, +0.57, +0.63, +0.76, +0.76, +0.81, +0.88, +0.99, +1.31 and +1.79. Seven of twelve beat the real result. A finding must clear the shuffled maximum, not the shuffled mean.

The one thing with a pulse

Gap continuation with an ADR-0.3 trail — and why it is still not tradeable

One configuration from Sweep A held up out of sample: enter in the direction of the overnight gap when it exceeds a quarter of average daily range, stop 30 points, trail at 0.3 × ADR, break-even at half-risk.

Three things disqualify it from live capital, and I would rather name them than bury them.

It dies on cost. PF 1.154 at 2.0 points, 1.080 at 2.5, 1.013 at 3.0, and 0.893 at 4.0. The entire result lives inside an assumption about your spread that has never been measured.

The exit is a spike, not a plateau. The stop dimension behaves well — ADR 0.5, ADR 0.75, fixed 20 and fixed 30 all land between PF 1.14 and 1.16. The exit dimension does not: every alternative sits at PF 1.00 (1R 1.008, 2R 1.016, 3R 1.011, ADR-0.5 target 0.982, time exit 0.991) and only trail 0.3 × ADR reaches 1.154. When one setting carries the whole result and its neighbours are flat, that is the shape of a fitted parameter.

The Monte Carlo is conditional on that cost. Ten thousand bootstrap resamples give a 92.8% probability of profit at 2.0 points — and 54.8% at 3.0, barely a coin flip. The probability of a drawdown reaching 15% of net profit is 100% at both.

What I could not do, and why

The spread is still unmeasured. Every cost figure in this report is an assumption. The gap-continuation result flips from viable to worthless between 2.0 and 3.0 points, so the single highest-value next step is a Dukascopy bid/ask download for AU200 around 10:00 Melbourne. Nothing else changes as much per hour spent.

09:50 coverage is unverified against your feed. My exports carry the pre-open bar on 11% of sessions; yours clearly carries it more often. Sweep B's sample of 173 sessions is a floor, not the true one.

TradingView is unreachable from here. The MCP server addresses localhost and this session runs in an isolated cloud container, so your running desktop is not visible to it regardless.

Where this leaves the strategy

The 10 AM Body Break is closed on AU200. Not because one configuration failed, but because the object it is built on — the direction of the 10:00 candle — has been measured directly and carries no information at any horizon tested. Stops, targets, trails, body filters, session cutoffs and flip logic are all transformations of a signal whose expected value is zero, and a transformation of zero is zero.

The gap-continuation result is the only direction worth another hour, and only after the spread is measured. If the true round-trip cost is near 2 points it becomes worth a forward test; if it is near 3, it is already dead and the measurement will have saved the effort.
