**Sources.** Five FXStreet.com webinars, Feb 2010 → Jan 2011, archived verbatim
at `sources/raw_transcripts/yotov_quarters_theory_webinar{1..5}_*.txt`. The

**Analysed 2026-08-23.**

## Verdict up front

**Two systems are being sold as one, and they need separate verdicts.**

| layer | webinars | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **The premise** — round 250-pip grid is special | 1, 2 | **Not falsifiable as stated.** Both branches exhaust the sample space. What can be extracted reduces to the x/S identity this repo already proved (H99). |
| **Trend Waves** — his Elliott modification | 3 | **Ordinary swing-structure analysis, renamed** — and genuinely improved by removing the sub-wave escape hatch. Nothing to do with quarters. |
| **Hesitation Zone system** | 4, 5 | **A real, mechanical, ex-ante breakout system that this repo has never tested.** May well work. Its author's explanation for *why* is the untested part. |

**The one thing to do about it:** the repo has refuted a *neighbouring* claim on a
*different* market. Run the measurement in §8 before saying anything stronger.

## 2. The complete system, assembled

No single webinar states the whole thing. Across all five:

### The grid
- **Major whole number** — 1.30, 1.40, 0.90, parity. Every 10 cents / 10 yen.
- **1000-pip range** — between two major whole numbers.
- **Large quarter** — 250 pips. **Large quarter point** — 1.3000, 1.3250, 1.3500 …
- **Whole number** — every 100 pips. **Small quarter** — 25 pips.
- Grid is **never re-anchored**. Asked directly in w5 whether to shift the range
  after a breakout: *"the answer is no … you cannot change math."*

### Premise (w1, w2)
Every significant move begins at a quarter point and ends at a quarter point.
Failure to complete → reversal to the **preceding** quarter point.

### Tolerances (w1)
- **Completion**: within **25 pips** of the target = completed.
- **Decisive breakout**: must exceed **25 pips** past a level, else "just an overshoot".

### Trend Waves (w3)
Reversal Trigger Wave (1) → Progressive (2) → Conclusive (3) → Consecutive (4+).
Corrections named **C1/C2/C3** for the wave they follow. No sub-waves, no complex
waves, no cap on count. >3 waves extended, >4 overextended. **Two mechanical
reversal triggers:** (i) a wave fails to exceed the prior same-direction extreme;
(ii) a correction exceeds the **100% retracement** of the preceding wave.

### Hesitation Zone (w4, w5) — the operational core
- **Hesitation Zone** — the first **75 pips** past a quarter point, in the
  direction of travel.
- **Decisive entry** — penetrate to the **far edge** (75 pips). ≤25 pips is "just
  an overshoot"; 31 pips he explicitly calls not decisive.
- **Pullback test** — price returns to the quarter point; it must **hold on the
  new side**.
- **Half point** — 125 pips, next target after the zone clears.
- **Last problematic price point** — the **whole number preceding** the target
  quarter point (1.4300 before 1.4250; 1.0200 before 1.0250).
- **Dual Hesitation Zone** — oscillating through both zones = indecision, no trade.

### 1000-pip range transition (w2, w5) — two requirements, both needed
1. **Complete the first large quarter** of the new 1000-pip range.
2. **On any pullback, hold beyond the major quarter point.**

Plus a discretionary gate: *"there has to be strong enough fundamental reason …
the underlying fundamentals must always justify"* the new range.

### Time stop (w4, glossary)
**Three-Day Rule** — three 24-hour sessions to complete a large quarter. Not
completed → time stop, prepare for reversal.

**Assembled, that is a complete mechanical trading system**: entry trigger,
confirmation, target, intermediate checkpoints, invalidation, time stop.
Webinars 1–2 alone are not. Webinars 4–5 are what make it one.

## 3. The part that cannot be falsified

The premise makes no prediction, and he says so himself without noticing:

> *"here is the beauty ladies and gentlemen — the outcome of both events, whether
> a large quarter is completed or whether it is not successfully completed, the
> outcome of both events always leads to a price move that targets a familiar
> price level."*

The branches exhaust the sample space. Price either travels 250 pips — and
therefore arrives at the adjacent grid line, **by arithmetic**, since the spacing
*is* 250 — or it does not, and if it retraces it heads back toward the line it
came from. No observation can contradict this. It describes a partition of the
number line.

**This repo already formalised the maths.** Belief **H99**: for a driftless walk
starting *x* above the bottom of an interval of width *S*, P(top before bottom) =
***x/S* exactly**. Starting *at* a boundary — where every Quarters Theory move
begins by definition — gives 0.5 either way, on **any** grid, in **any** market,
and in simulated noise. Measured on gold's $25 grid: 24.0 vs 20.0, 43.9 vs 40.0,
53.6 vs 50.0, 63.0 vs 60.0, 81.1 vs 80.0 — the identity, within a few points.

Everything in §2 from the Hesitation Zone onward is, by contrast, **falsifiable**.
That distinction is the whole analysis.

## 4. His own numbers, scored against his own rules

### 4a. The 25-pip completion rule

Every price for which he explicitly invokes a quarter relationship:

| price | nearest quarter | pips | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.4257 | 1.4250 | 7 | HIT |
| 1.4556 | 1.4500 | **56** | MISS — offered as "approximately the half point" |
| 1.4028 | 1.4000 | **28** | MISS — offered as "almost completed the range" |
| 1.3444 | 1.3500 | **56** | MISS — offered as "approximately the major half point" |
| 1.3267 | 1.3250 | 17 | HIT |
| 1.2506 | 1.2500 | 6 | HIT |
| 1.3093 | 1.3000 | **93** | MISS — offered as "even overshoots above" |
| 1.2758 | 1.2750 | 8 | HIT |
| 1.2517 | 1.2500 | 17 | HIT |
| 1.2234 | 1.2250 | 16 | HIT |
| 1.4281 | 1.4250 | **31** | MISS — *he correctly calls this one not decisive* |
| 1.0002 | 1.0000 | 2 | HIT |
| 1.0182 | 1.0250 | **68** | MISS — the failed AUD/USD high |
| 80.23 | 80.00 | 23 | HIT |

**8 of 14 = 57%.** Base rate is **20.0%** — the ±25-pip band is 50 of every 250
pips, so one price in five "completes a quarter" by chance.

Both things are true and they point opposite ways:

- 57% is well above 20% (binomial p = 0.0024 *if* this were a fair sample).
- **It is not a fair sample.** It is what an advocate selected to persuade an
  audience — the ceiling of cherry-picking, not an estimate. And **six of the
  fourteen he presented as confirmations fail his own rule**, one (1.3093) by 93
  pips, nearly four times the tolerance.

One clean instance of the reference switching when the grid misses: EUR/USD at
1.2353 is 103 pips from the nearest quarter point, so he measures it against
**1.2328, the 2008 swing low** instead. That is the human form of **BUG-047**,
already registered here from Pine code — *a rejected signal that does not consume
the day becomes a search for one that passes.* A level that fails to explain a
price is quietly replaced by one that does.

### 4b. The hesitation-zone rule — the first genuinely ex-ante calls

| setup | penetration | decisive? | outcome | rule right? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EUR/USD above 1.4250 | 31 pips | no | reversed to 1.4000 | ✓ |
| EUR/USD below 1.4250 | 75 pips | yes | completed to 1.4000 | ✓ |
| EUR/USD below 1.4000 | 75 pips | yes | completed to 1.3750 | ✓ |
| GBP/USD above 1.6250 | 49 pips | no | reversed | ✓ |
| GBP/USD below 1.6250 | 75 pips | yes | completed to 1.6000 | ✓ |
| AUD/USD above 1.0000 | 75 pips | yes | **failed** at 1.0182 | ✗ |

**5 of 6** — hand-picked, so it proves nothing, but note he *shows the failure*,
which is more than webinars 1–3 do.

### 4c. The accretion pattern — the corpus-level finding

Visible only with all five webinars in hand:

| webinar | new test introduced | the case it explains, narrated in the same session |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | trend-wave failure; 100% retracement flip | the Aug-2010 EUR/USD cycle |
| 4 | Hesitation Zone; decisive entry; **"last problematic price point"** | Nov-2010 AUD/USD failing at 1.0182 |
| 5 | two-part transition test (complete **and** hold) | Dec-2010 AUD/USD completing 1.0250 then losing parity |

**Every new test arrives alongside the case it explains, and none was stated
before the event.** Sharper still: the *"last problematic price point"* is
introduced in w4 specifically to explain why AUD/USD stalled at 1.0182 — and when
w5 re-narrates that identical November event, the concept is **not mentioned**.
It survives elsewhere as a checkpoint in the target ladder, but as an explanation
it appears exactly once, when needed. That is a disposable parameter, and
disposable parameters are how a framework stops being testable.

## 5. The forward calls — the only out-of-sample evidence, and it favours him

Webinar 5 (Fri 21 Jan 2011) makes three dated, checkable calls. Unlike everything
else in the corpus these were made *before* the outcome.

| pair | call | what happened | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AUD/USD** | transition confirmed only if it re-completes 1.0250 **and** holds above parity on the pullback; then advance toward 1.05 | completed 1.0250 Feb–Mar 2011, held above parity Mar–Sep, ran to 1.1081 on 27 Jul 2011 | **correct** |
| **EUR/USD** | breaking above 1.3500, targeting 1.3750, wants pullbacks to hold 1.3500 | cleared 1.3750 within ~2 weeks, on to 1.4939 by 4 May 2011 | **correct** |
| **EUR/CHF** | 50% of the 1,400-pip decline ≈ 700 pips, toward 1.30–1.31 | rallied ~1.2400 → ~1.3200 by early Feb 2011, ~800 pips | **correct** |

**3 of 3.** That must be reported; it is the only out-of-sample evidence here and
it cuts his way. Three caveats, all material:

1. **Regime.** 2011 H1 was a persistent USD downtrend across every major (QE2 ran
   to June). A rule that says *break a level, target the next level 250 pips on*
   wins in exactly that regime. Three correct calls in one trending window is
   consistent with "competent momentum system" and **does not discriminate
   round grid from any grid**.
2. **Selection.** These are the calls he chose to make on a webinar. There is no
   record of the calls he didn't make, or the ones that failed.
3. **The EUR/CHF call is not a quarters call at all.** He computes it explicitly
   as 50% of a 1,400-pip decline — a Fibonacci retracement. The quarters
   framework contributes nothing beyond 1.30 being a round number.

> **Provenance warning.** These 2011 outcomes are from the historical record as
> recalled, **not** verified against primary data held in this repo (XAUUSD ticks
> only, 2024–2026). Verify before relying on them.

## 6. What this repo already knows, and what it does not

**Bears on the claim:**

- **H99 / GL-series** — the acceptance ladder is *x/S*. Kills the w1–w2 premise
  mathematically, in every market. Not gold-specific.
- **H98** — roundness does nothing *on gold*. Across S ∈ {1, 2.5, 5, 10, 12.5,
  25, 50, 100} the round phase's traverse advantage is ≤ +0.0106 and changes
  sign; time near levels is ~33.3% everywhere.
- **GL06** — key levels and pivots sit at their controls; best edge +0.6pp, and
  the best of eight *sham* levels beats it with p = 0.998.
- **GL03** — the phase-shift control is the right experiment: holding a rule
  byte-identical and moving only the grid phase, 5 of 24 non-round grids beat the
  round one.
- **H100 / GH-series** — the $25 quarter filter fails out of sample on gold:
  halves the trade count, triples drawdown (80.5 → 167.5), turns an unseen year
  negative (1.145 → 0.675).

**Scope limits — state these plainly:**

1. **This repo tested gold. Yotov claims FX.** Every empirical result above is
   XAUUSD. His mechanism ("we all use the base-10 numeral system") would apply to
   any market — and in w5 he says he is extending it to *"stocks indices
   commodities futures"* — but the repo has **never run the round-grid test on
   EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY or AUD/USD**. The x/S maths transfers; the gold data
   does not.
2. **The repo tested a different construction.** Phase 6 measured *traverse and
   acceptance statistics* and *rejection on first touch*. It never tested a
   **penetration-confirmed breakout with a fixed grid target and a time stop** —
   which is what §2 specifies. Different experiment, untouched.

So: **the repo's work refutes the premise and is silent on the system.**

## 7. AMDM reading (standing rule)

- **Model 1 (momentum-join)** — decisive break of a quarter point → target the
  next quarter point 250 pips on.
- **Model 2 (mean-reversion)** — failed completion → reverse to the preceding
  quarter point.

Webinars 1–2 run both with **no ex-ante arbiter**; which model applies is decided
after the outcome is known. Under AMDM that is not a system — the model selection
*is* the edge, and there it is hindsight.

**Webinars 3–5 supply the missing arbiter**, and this is the most useful thing in
the five hours:

| observation | model selected | ex ante? |
|---|---|---|
| penetration ≥75 pips, pullback holds | **Model 1** — expect completion | yes |
| penetration ≤25 pips ("overshoot") | **Model 2** — expect reversal | yes |
| trend wave fails to exceed prior extreme | **Model 2** | yes |
| correction exceeds 100% retracement | **regime flip** | yes |
| dual hesitation zone | no trade | yes |
| first quarter of a new range completes **and** pullback holds | **Model 1**, range-scale | yes |
| 3 days elapsed, quarter incomplete | **Model 2** — time stop | yes |
| *"strong enough fundamental reason"* | — | **no — discretionary, unfalsifiable** |

Every mechanical row is computable at decision time. **None requires the grid to
be round.** All are distances from a grid line — 25 / 75 / 125 / 250 — and every
rule still computes on a grid shifted to any phase. That is where the test goes.

The last row is the exception and it is load-bearing: the fundamental-justification
gate can retroactively excuse any failed transition. It should be dropped before
testing, not tested.

## 8. The test that settles it

Run the **complete §2 system** — decisive-entry filter, pullback hold, 250-pip
target, 125-pip half point, 3-day time stop — on the round 250-pip grid, then on
**24 phase-shifted grids of identical spacing** (offsets 10, 20 … 240 pips). Same
rule, same data, same costs; only the phase moves.

- Round grid beats the shifted distribution → **roundness is real**, and it would
  be the first result in this repo to say so.
- Round grid sits inside the distribution → he has built a **decent
  penetration-confirmed breakout system** and misattributed its edge to the
  number system. The system may still be worth trading; the theory of *why* is
  wrong.
- Neither beats a no-trade baseline after spread → it is a coordinate system.

**Priority order:**

1. **The completion asymmetry — do this first.** Conditional on price sitting at
   a quarter point, measure P(traverse 250 pips before retracing 250 pips). H99
   predicts **0.5**. Any real edge must beat 0.5. No strategy, no parameters, no
   costs — a pure measurement, one afternoon on tick data. It decides whether §3
   is merely tautological or actually false.
2. **Phase-shift control on FX majors** — EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, AUD/USD.
   Closes the gold-only scope gap. Requires downloading FX ticks; the Dukascopy
   pipeline in `research/gold_tick_data/` already does this and only needs the
   symbol changed.
3. **The 25-pip tolerance as a free parameter** — sweep 0–75 pips. If 25 is
   special there is a peak; if the curve just rises with tolerance, the number is
   arbitrary and the "hit rate" is a definition.
4. **The 75-pip decisive filter** against a no-filter control and against the
   same filter on shifted grids.
5. **The 3-day rule as a time stop**, independently, on round and shifted grids.
   Note Phase 4 here found time stops *destroyed* a gold entry universe (15m →
   PF 0.77) — but a 3-day stop on a 250-pip target is a different animal and
   deserves its own test.

## 9. Verdict

**The premise (w1–w2): not falsifiable as stated.** The "beauty" he names — that
both branches lead to a familiar level — is the tell. Grids produce grid-line
arrivals because that is what grids do.

**Trend Waves (w3): swing structure, renamed, and modestly improved.**
"Correction exceeds the 100% retracement" is a break of structure; "higher highs
to continue an uptrend" is the definition of an uptrend. Removing Elliott's wave
cap and sub-wave escape hatch makes the count reproducible — a real if small
contribution. Unrelated to quarters.

**Hesitation Zone system (w4–w5): a real, mechanical, testable breakout system
this repo has never tested.** A 75-pip penetration filter with a pullback-hold
confirmation, a 250-pip target, intermediate checkpoints and a 3-day time stop is
a perfectly reasonable trend-following spec. It might work. Its author believes
it works *because of the base-10 number system*; that attribution is what the
phase-shift control kills or confirms, and it is independent of whether the
system makes money.

**The man: an accurate observer and an unreliable inferrer.** Prices right to the
pip across four pairs and fifteen months, dates right, feed discrepancies
volunteered, failures sometimes shown, three forward calls correct. And: six of
fourteen self-selected examples counted as confirmations while failing his own
tolerance; one reference switched mid-argument when the grid missed; a new test
introduced at each webinar alongside the case it explains; one explanatory
concept used once and silently dropped; and a discretionary fundamental gate that
can excuse any failure.

**What to do.** Do not adopt the premise. Do not promote any of this to the
playbook. **Do** run test 1 — it is cheap, it is decisive about §3, and the
honest position right now is that this repo has refuted a *neighbouring* claim on
a *different* market and has not tested the one in front of it.
