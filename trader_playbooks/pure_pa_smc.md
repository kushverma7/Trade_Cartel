## 1. STRONG / WEAK STRUCTURAL POINTS [genuinely new, precise rule]
- A structural high/low is **STRONG** only if price later breaks
  through the most recent OPPOSITE-type S/D zone (e.g. a low is
  strong if the subsequent rally breaks the last supply zone).
- A structural point is **WEAK** if price approaches it and rejects
  without breaking that opposite zone.
- **Non-retroactive rule**: breaking a WEAK low does NOT retroactively
  make the WEAK high before it strong. Weakness only clears forward,
  never backward -- a specific, easy-to-get-wrong mechanic worth
  encoding explicitly.
- Distinct from Dave's swing-maturity count (H18, counts swings) and
  Alchemist's IDM (gates BOS on a swept inducement) -- this classifies
  swing points by whether they later interact successfully with an
  OPPOSING S/D ZONE specifically, a third distinct lens on structure
  quality.

## 2. THE THREE-FACTOR S/D ZONE CHECKLIST [genuinely new]
A valid, tradeable S/D zone requires ALL THREE:
1. **IFC (Inefficiency / Imbalance / Fair Value Gap)**: the impulse
   leaving the zone left behind a genuine 3-candle gap (candle 1's
   extreme doesn't overlap candle 3's extreme, candle 2 is the gap).
2. **Structure break / CHoCH**: the impulse actually broke structure
   or changed character, not just moved.
3. **"Did it raise money"** (their phrasing for a liquidity grab): the
   move captured resting liquidity (took out equal highs/lows, a
   trendline liquidity pool, or a prior swing extreme) on the way.
Missing even ONE factor = lower probability, skip. Different checklist
axis than Wendell's freshness/candle-count/departure-speed (H30) --
this is about WHAT THE IMPULSE LEFT BEHIND, not how the base formed.
Always use the MOST RECENT zone satisfying all three -- older zones
satisfying the criteria are lower priority even if "cleaner" looking.

## 3. MITIGATED vs UNMITIGATED ZONES
- A zone is unmitigated until price returns and taps it once.
- Entries AND take-profit targets should both be unmitigated zones --
  using an already-tapped zone as a TP target is explicitly called out
  as a mistake.
- Same underlying concept as Wendell's zone-freshness (H30) but
  independently arrived at, and extended to TP SELECTION as well as
  entries -- Wendell's source doesn't make that TP-side extension
  explicit. Logged as corroboration of H30 with one added nuance.

## 4. "WHO'S IN CONTROL" — regime by last zone-interaction winner
- Track which side (supply or demand) most recently WON a zone
  interaction: if price broke through the last supply zone, demand is
  now "in control"; if the last supply zone REJECTED price back down,
  supply retains control.
- Trade WITH the controlling side. Don't take the non-controlling
  side without a confirmed FLIP (see section 5).
- Marked visually: light red = supply in control, light blue = demand
  in control.
- Distinct from every other regime-classification idea already in the
  register (PBD balance/imbalance, EMA-stack bias, HTF bias in FX
  Master Pattern) -- this one is defined purely by WHO WON THE LAST
  ZONE FIGHT, a genuinely different construct.

## 5. SUPPLY/DEMAND FLIPS
- Price makes a run at a new extreme, taps the opposing zone (e.g. the
  "last demand zone" during an up-move), FAILS to make a fresh higher
  high, then impulsively breaks back down THROUGH that demand zone --
  leaving a new supply zone behind and flipping control.
- Best quality when the reversal move is aggressive (few large candles
  breaking the zone quickly, leaving IFC behind) rather than grinding.
- Related to Quasimodo (Alchemist, voice #12 -- failed reclaim of a
  prior extreme) and PBD's b-FAIL pattern, but framed specifically
  around ZONE CONTROL flipping rather than pure swing geometry --
  logged as a related convergence, not a duplicate.

## 6. CHoCH vs CONTINUATION ENTRIES
- **CHoCH entry**: the primary reversal trade. Best quality when the
  CHoCH move crosses AT LEAST TWO S/D zones in one impulsive push with
  large candles -- a single-zone CHoCH is lower quality.
- **Continuation entry**: for missing the CHoCH/flip -- either (a)
  scale in on a pullback to a fresh zone once the new direction is
  established, targeting the next unmitigated opposing zone, or (b)
  enter when price reaches and breaks a HTF zone in the already-
  established direction.
- **Liquidity grab vs BOS geometry**: a liquidity grab is typically a
  SINGLE impulsive candle with a large, dominant wick; a real BOS is a
  body-close break, often over multiple candles. Distinguishing these
  by candle shape (wick-dominant single spike vs body-close-confirmed
  multi-candle break) is a concrete, codeable rule -- converges with
  Steve/MMM4x's wick-vs-body-close distinction (voice #8) from an
  unrelated lineage.

## 7. LIQUIDITY
- Stop losses ARE liquidity; liquidity exists wherever stops cluster.
- Primary liquidity pools: equal/double/triple highs and lows,
  trendline liquidity (a series of higher lows or lower highs that
  retail traders trail stops beneath/above).
- Marked with "$$$" on charts.

## 8. EQUILIBRIUM (50%) ENTRY RULE
- If the zone-forming candle's wick is more than 50% of its total
  range, OR if a max stop distance (their case: 1.5-2 pips, an
  extremely tight scalp stop) cannot cover the full zone, enter at the
  50% midpoint of the zone instead of its edge. Purely an execution
  refinement for tight-stop precision trading.

## 9. MULTI-TIMEFRAME WORKFLOW (this source's full daily process)
1. Check high-impact news for the session (ForexFactory-style
   calendar); avoid opening new trades into a red-folder release;
   existing positions go to breakeven before the release.
2. **H4**: do NOT trade off it directly -- only use it to determine
   which side (supply/demand) is in control, i.e. the macro direction
   and HTF target zone.
3. **15m**: mark supply/demand zones satisfying the three-factor
   checklist (section 2).
4. **5m**: refine the 15m zone down to a tighter, cleaner boundary.
5. **1m**: refine again; if the 1m gives two unmitigated zones inside
   the 5m zone, prefer the still-unmitigated one; if the 5m zone
   itself is clean (no sub-zones), keep it as-is.
6. Choose entry type (S/D zone tap / S-D flip / CHoCH continuation)
   with the liquidity-grab confirmation layer.
7. Manage the trade targeting the next unmitigated opposing zone.
This is the SEVENTH independent voice on the "HTF sets bias, LTF gives
the precision entry" MTF-stack idea already dominant in the register
(now 7 sources across 7 different mechanics).

## 10. RISK / EXECUTION RULES (operational, not market-structure)
- 1% risk per trade; hard stop at -2% for the day, then stop trading
  and backtest the mistakes instead.
- Flat before session end every day -- never hold across sessions.
  Independently corroborates Steve/MMM4x's (voice #8) identical rule.
- Immediate move to breakeven ahead of any high-impact news release if
  already in a position -- same spirit as, but more precise than, the
  Forex James broker-risk source's general volatility caution.
- Extremely tight fixed stops (1.5-2 pips) matched to precision-
  refined 1m zones -- implies this style only works with genuinely
  tight, well-refined entries; not a generic stop-sizing rule to copy
  onto looser setups.
- **Spread buffering**: when spreads widen, buffer BOTH the entry
  limit and the stop loss by the spread amount, to avoid missed fills
  or premature stop-outs. Direct, practical execution hygiene --
  logged alongside the earlier Forex James broker-risk notes in
  PLAYBOOK.md's Execution/Broker Risk section.
