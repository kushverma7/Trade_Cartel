# PART 1: THE MODEL

## 1.1 The CLC Rule — The Complete Framework

Every single trade must satisfy all three pillars simultaneously:

**C — Context**

- Who is aggressive right now?
- Who is trapped (wrong-way participants)?
- Is volume coherent with price direction?
- What is the higher timeframe telling you about the range?
- Context = understanding the narrative before touching a chart

**L — Location**

- Is price at a meaningful level of interest?
- Low volume node, zero print, HVN breakout, previous session high/low
- If it’s not at a level, there is no business transacting
- “I don’t look at the market moving down or up as missing it. I just look at it as me not participating because I have no reason to”

**C — Confirmation**

- Aggressive buying/selling at the level
- Absorption (heavy volume, minimal price movement)
- Speed of tape picking up at the level
- Delta outlier in footprint at level
- All three must appear in alignment — not just one or two

> “When all three align — context fitting, location at a good level, valid confirmation — that’s when a good setup needs risk put on.”

-----

## 1.2 Zero Prints — The Unique Edge

**Definition:** Price levels where one side of the market transacted zero volume.

How they form:

- Market moves aggressively in one direction
- At certain prices, no trades hit the bid (aggressive move up)
- OR no trades hit the offer (aggressive move down)
- These prices never had a fair opportunity for the other side to trade

**Why they matter:**

- Market is an efficiency-seeking mechanism
- Unvisited prices will always be revisited at some point
- Could be same session, next day, or next week — but they get filled
- The further price moves from the zero print, the better the setup when it returns
- “The likelihood of them filling is pretty high”

**How to use:**

- Mark immediately when formed
- When price returns to fill them = entry opportunity
- Use as TP target for trades in that direction
- Pair with absorption at the level for highest probability

**Live example from session:**

- Pre-market lows broke → zero prints formed at 6820 and 6827
- Price filled both within 30 seconds of NY open
- “That would have been a fantastic long opportunity” — A+ setup per Carmine
- Very low risk, asymmetric reward

-----

## 1.3 Volume Profile — How Carmine Uses It

**Session profile (cash only):**

- Reset at NY open — uses cash session volume only for intraday levels
- Cash session profile more sensitive than full/overnight session
- Levels from cash session = where intraday traders actually transacted

**For higher timeframe analysis:**

- Merges multiple sessions into composite profile
- Identifies long-term balance areas and imbalances
- HVN = significant acceptance, choppy if price returns there
- LVN = line of least resistance, fast-moving zone

**Profile shapes:**

- P-shape (volume at top, thin at bottom) = bullish accumulation, look for longs
- B-shape (volume at bottom, thin at top) = bearish distribution, look for shorts
- Balanced bell curve = avoid — both sides transacting efficiently, choppy

**Key quote:**

> “I like trading where there’s not a lot of liquidity because that is where in my opinion a lot of the edge comes from.”

-----

## 1.4 Speed of Tape — The Confirmation Tool

**What it is:** Rate of incoming orders — how fast transactions are executing.

**What it tells you:**

- Normal pace = no urgency, market building position or choppy
- Picks up significantly = initiative — one side pressing hard
- Peak at a level + no follow-through = absorption = reversal signal
- Sustained increase = momentum building = continuation

**How he uses it:**

- Watch speed at KEY LEVELS only (not constantly)
- When speed of tape picks up at his level of interest = CLC confirmation
- “If I miss the speed of tape, I would have missed what happened there”
- Speed drops after initial move = squeeze is done, don’t add more

**Combined signal:**

- Large delta outlier in footprint + speed of tape at level = A+ confirmation
- “Speed of tape, aggression, and absorption coherence in one candle = all three in one”

-----

## 1.5 Footprint Chart — The Primary Execution Tool

**Timeframe:** 20-tick range bars on ES

**What he reads:**

- Shape of volume distribution within each bar
- Delta column: net buy minus sell at each price level
- Outlier delta = significant participant interest at that price
- Large accumulation at low of bar = absorption (buyers absorbing sellers)
- P-shaped bar = bullish (volume clustering at top)
- Hammer-shaped bar = potential reversal (long lower wick)

**Key concept:** When bars open more frequently = volatility/speed increasing. When bar takes 20 minutes to open = very choppy, low volatility.

-----

## 1.6 The Advertising Mechanism (Market Philosophy)

> “The market is an advertising mechanism. If you’re looking to buy something, you want to buy it on discount. If the market’s on sale and nobody’s buying it, why should we buy it if nobody’s buying it?”

The order flow tells you:

- When something goes on discount, are people buying?
- If yes → follow the footsteps
- If no → the discount isn’t actually attractive to participants
- This is why watching volume at levels matters more than the price level alone

-----

## 1.7 Intermarket Analysis (Carmine’s Use)

**When he uses it:**

- Only when there is a significant outlier between markets
- NQ significantly up while ES does nothing → look at NQ for rejection/resistance, play opposite
- When they move in sync → no edge in comparing them

**His conclusion:**

- NQ is less liquid = moves first, sets bias for ES
- ES is more liquid = slower, tends to mean-revert more
- ES = better for mean reversion model
- NQ = better for trend following (Fabio’s instrument choice confirmed here)

**Key insight:** After 2 consecutive strong directional days → expect balance/digestion. Don’t look for trend continuation.

-----

## 2.2 Entry Types

**Type 1: Mean Reversion at Level (Preferred)**

- Market moves to level of interest
- Zero print or LVN at that level
- Absorption visible (large volume, no follow-through)
- Speed of tape picks up on reversal side
- Enter immediately when delta outlier appears
- Target = opposite side of range, zero print, or balance center

**Type 2: Continuation / Market Structure Demand**

- Market breaks a significant level with aggression
- Fixed range volume profile shows strong buying into the break
- No LVN directly above (clear path)
- Wait for pullback to “market structure demand” = breakout level
- Buyers defending that level on retrace
- Target = next zero print, balance area, or higher time frame level

**Type 3: Reload Setup**

- Market trends in one direction
- Buyers step in aggressively at a dip (large delta, speed of tape)
- If they hold (don’t get absorbed by sellers) = reload opportunity
- Small tight stop, high probability continuation

**What he never does:**

- Trade in the middle of a range
- Chase a move that already happened
- Enter without CLC all aligned
- Trade same level same direction more than 3 times

-----

## 2.3 Stop Loss and Trade Management

**Stop loss:**

- Always placed immediately on entry
- Standard trades: just beyond the level of interest
- On ES: can keep very tight due to liquidity (40-50 contracts book depth)
- Move to BE when trade moves 7-10 points favorably
- “If it moves in my favor relatively quickly, I’ll adjust to break even”

**Never:**

- Widen stop hoping for recovery
- Add to losing position (no Martingale)
- Hold through confirmed invalidation

**Position sizing:**

- Standardized dollar risk (~$2,500 per trade)
- Same amount regardless of setup type
- If stop is tighter → larger position size
- If stop is wider → smaller position size
- “I standardize my risk, keep my risk the same dollar amount”

**Trade management philosophy:**

- Best trades work out IMMEDIATELY (under 5-7 minutes on ES)
- Worst trades = stuck for 1-2 hours
- If trade hangs around at entry for too long = exit, setup weakening
- “A good trade works out right away”

-----

## 2.4 Three-Loss Rule

If he takes the same trade (same analysis, same level) 3 times and loses all 3:

- Stop trading that idea completely
- The analysis is wrong — market doesn’t agree
- Move on, look for different setup
- “Those are my biggest losing days when I go against that rule”

-----

## 2.5 What He Never Trades

- Moves not at a level of interest (“I don’t see the market moving as me missing it”)
- Middle of range with no structural reason
- Choppy/balanced markets with trend model
- After 3 consecutive losses on same level
- When CLC not fully aligned
- When speed of tape is flat/low
- Post-expansion digestion days with trend continuation model
- Anything he cannot immediately label/journal

> “If I can’t tell myself what trade I’m taking and give it a label, I have no business taking it.”

- Worst trades: last 1-2 hours
- Min R:R accepted: 2:1
