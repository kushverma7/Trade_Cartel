## TOPIC 1: THE EXISTENCE OF A HIDDEN GLOBAL PLAN ("THE GREAT WORK")

### Cooper's Core Claim

- A centuries-old blueprint exists called "The Great Work," "The Great Plan," or "The Lost Word"
- This plan is deliberately unfolding across centuries
- Most people never notice because change occurs gradually
- The plan involves establishing a world government with a figurehead ruler and a council of "wise men" who hold real power

### Historical Context

- The term "Great Work" has roots in Hermetic and occult philosophy
- Historical secret societies (Freemasons, Illuminati, Rosicrucians) have written about grand plans for civilization
- The concept gained modern traction through 20th-century conspiracy literature

### Credible Sources for Research

1. **Adam Weishaupt and the Bavarian Illuminati** - Primary source documents (1776)
   - Original founding documents available through academic archives
   - *Recommended:* "The Illuminati Conspiracy" - academic papers by academic historians
2. **Secret Societies and Historical Power Structures** by Nesta Webster
   - Early 20th-century analysis of secret society influence
   - Controversial but historically documented membership records exist
3. **Masonic Records and Archives**
   - Publicly available membership records (many lodges publish this)
   - Pike's "Morals and Dogma" - official Freemasonic text explaining degrees
4. **Council on Foreign Relations (CFR) - Official Publications**
   - CFR publishes membership rosters publicly
   - Their journal "Foreign Affairs" is publicly accessible
   - Official CFR statements on global governance are documented

### Current Research Status

- **Documented Fact:** Elite networks DO coordinate policy (CFR, Trilateral Commission, WEF are real organizations with published memberships and agendas)
- **Contested Elements:** Whether this coordination constitutes a "hidden plan" dating back centuries vs. institutional continuity of power structures
- **Academic Sources:** Scholars like C. Wright Mills ("The Power Elite") and G. William Domhoff ("Who Rules America?") document actual power coordination without invoking centuries-old blueprints

### Critical Assessment

- [YES] Real organizations exist and coordinate policy
- [NO] Evidence for centuries-old unified plan is circumstantial, based on symbol interpretation
- [?] Whether coordination = conscious conspiracy vs. institutional self-interest

## TOPIC 3: THE CAPITOL BUILDING'S MASONIC AND PAGAN SYMBOLISM

### Cooper's Core Claim

- The U.S. Capitol dome contains the "Apotheosis of Washington" - depicting Washington as a Roman god ascending to heaven
- The dome is ringed with figures from Greek and Roman mythology (Zeus, Mercury, Prometheus, etc.)
- This proves the U.S. was founded as a Masonic/deist nation, NOT a Christian nation
- The government is spending $60 million preserving this "pagan" imagery

### What's Factually Documented

**The Apotheosis of Washington Fresco:**

- [YES] Painted by Constantino Brumidi (1865)
- [YES] Located in the Capitol rotunda dome (largest fresco in U.S. at ~4,664 square feet)
- [YES] Depicts Washington ascending to heaven with Roman/mythological figures below
- [YES] Surrounded by personifications of Liberty, Science, Industry, etc. - NOT saints or biblical figures
- Source: U.S. Capitol Historical Society (official)

**The Mythological Figures:**

- [YES] Apollo, Minerva, Mercury, Vulcan, Neptune visible in the fresco
- [YES] These are classical/Enlightenment references, not Christian iconography
- Source: Capitol dome fresco scholarly documentation

**Recent Restoration:**

- [YES] The Capitol dome underwent major restoration ($60 million) 2014-2016
- [YES] Over 1,000 cracks were repaired
- [YES] Every detail of Brumidi's fresco was meticulously preserved
- Source: U.S. Capitol Visitor Center official documentation

### Historical Explanation

- Capitol architect was Thomas U. Walter (deist)
- Brumidi was an Italian Renaissance artist commissioned to paint in classical style
- The Apotheosis reflects Enlightenment ideals: Washington as leader of reason/secular government
- This was considered APPROPRIATE symbolism for a nation founded on reason, not theocracy

### Credible Sources for Study

1. **U.S. Capitol Historical Society** - Official documentation
   - "The Capitol Dome" - technical and historical analysis
   - Published fresco documentation
2. **Constantino Brumidi Scholarship**
   - *Brumidi: A Life* - academic biography
   - Restoration project documentation (2014-2016)
3. **Architectural History**
   - *The United States Capitol: Its History, Architecture, and Decoration* - official scholarly work
   - Library of Congress collections on Capitol design
4. **Art Historical Analysis**
   - Analysis of Enlightenment iconography in American architecture
   - Academic papers on separation of church and state in architectural design

### Critical Assessment

- [YES] The symbolism IS pagan/classical (not Christian)
- [YES] The government DID spend millions preserving it
- [YES] This reflects Enlightenment values, not necessarily a "conspiracy"
- [?] Does preservation of original symbolism prove an anti-Christian agenda, or simply respect for historical design?
- [NO] "Christian nationalism" debates of 2024+ don't change what the original symbols were

## TOPIC 4: THOMAS JEFFERSON AND THE JEFFERSON BIBLE

### Cooper's Core Claim

- Thomas Jefferson tore up the Bible with a razor
- He removed every miracle, supernatural event, and claim of divinity
- He created his own version called the Jefferson Bible
- This proves founding fathers weren't Christian
- The Jefferson Bible still exists at the Smithsonian

### What's Historically Documented

**The Jefferson Bible - Facts:**

- [YES] **Title:** "The Life and Morals of Jesus of Nazareth"
- [YES] **Method:** Jefferson used a razor to cut passages from King James Bible and other translations, then pasted them into a new order (1820)
- [YES] **Content:** Removed ALL miracles, resurrection accounts, and divinity claims; kept only ethical teachings of Jesus
- [YES] **Current Location:** Both original artifact and published facsimiles exist
  - Original at Smithsonian Institution (viewable)
  - Published versions available from Beacon Press, Dover Publications, etc.
- [YES] **Purpose:** Jefferson wanted to extract Jesus's moral philosophy divorced from supernatural claims

**Jefferson's Religious Views:**

- [YES] **Self-identified as "deist"** - believed in God but not organized religion
- [YES] **Criticized orthodox Christianity** - called it "incomprehensible" and based on "priestly fraud"
- [YES] **Advocated Separation of Church and State** - Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom (1786)
- [YES] **Wrote extensively** about his religious skepticism (available in letters)

### Credible Sources for Deep Study

1. **The Jefferson Bible Itself**
   - *The Life and Morals of Jesus of Nazareth: Extracted Textually from the Gospels* - published by Smithsonian Institution Press
   - Dover Publications edition (affordable, complete)
   - Beacon Press edition (includes scholarly introduction)
2. **Jefferson's Writings on Religion**
   - *The Adams-Jefferson Letters: Complete Correspondence Between Thomas Jefferson and John Adams*
     - Letters discussing religious philosophy
     - Available from University of North Carolina Press
   - *Jefferson's Letters* (selected letters on religion) - Library of Congress collection
3. **Scholarly Works**
   - *The Inner Jefferson: Portrait of a Grieving Optimist* - Andrew Burstein
     - Discusses Jefferson's personal philosophy including religious views
   - *Thomas Jefferson: An Intimate History* - Fawn Brodie
     - Detailed analysis of Jefferson's beliefs
   - *Jefferson and Religion* - academic papers in *Journal of American History*
4. **Documentary Sources**
   - Smithsonian Institution Exhibition Catalog on Jefferson Bible
   - American Philosophical Society (holds Jefferson papers)

### Current Historical Consensus

- [YES] **Established fact:** Jefferson created this document and removed supernatural elements
- [YES] **Established fact:** Jefferson was skeptical of organized Christianity
- [YES] **Established fact:** He was a deist, not an orthodox Christian
- [?] **Debated:** Whether one founding father's views represent all founding fathers' intentions

### Critical Assessment

- [YES] Jefferson absolutely removed miracles and divinity claims
- [YES] The document exists and is preserved
- [YES] His religious skepticism is well-documented
- [NO] Doesn't automatically prove the entire founding was "anti-Christian"
- [?] Other founders (John Adams, Benjamin Franklin) had different religious views than Jefferson
